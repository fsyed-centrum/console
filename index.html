Historylast 40 you copied
  • Structural beats descriptive. Lyric-sheet edits (line fragmentation, repeated vowels, physical repeats) force Suno's hand. Style tags are only hints it weighs.
  • Rhythm tags land. Harmony tags don't. Swing, shuffle, pocket, backbeat all work. Chord theory terms — Neapolitan, half-diminished, planing — did nothing.
  • Name the technique, not the person. An artist tag pulls their most famous work: Reznor → industrial, Nirvana → grunge, Bublé → big band.
  • Band-format anchors resist filmi drift. Production styles don't. Enigma, Alan Walker, Stereo Love and raï all collapsed; Radiohead, Coldplay and Stromae held.
  • Simple loop harmony is the drift condition. A four-chord minor vamp with Urdu lyrics goes filmi almost every time.
  • The first third of the prompt is what lands. Anything you can't afford to lose goes early; mood adjectives are expendable tail.
  • Small edits inside a working frame beat rebuilding. Three times now the fix was one tag, and the rebuild made it worse.
  • Suno always fills the lead role. Excluding an instrument just hands the job to the next one — acoustic guitar out, pedal steel in. Assign the role deliberately instead; restrictive tags like "never a lead line" don't hold in instrumentals.
  • Name the note value or you get sixteenths. "Held across whole bars", "slow eighth note arpeggio", "one or two per bar" — unstated means Suno's busy default.
  • Sixteenths in the hats are good; sixteenths in the synths and bass are clutter. Motion belongs in the kit, long values everywhere else.
  • Sparse by default. Your vocal and backing vocals fill the space — a bed that sounds complete alone is already too full.
  • Synths lack emotion. An acoustic instrument — piano, cello, strings — carries the emotional foundation under any synth arrangement.
  • Scope a vocal genre to the vocal layer ("Soul And Folk Vocal Delivery Over X Instrumentation") when the bare genre tag hijacks the arrangement. The bare form often works too — it's a coin flip, and the scoped form is the fallback.
  • Instruments carry behavioural priors from their genre home. Bass clarinet solos, pedal steel fills, acoustic guitar leads, church organ dominates. Say what it's doing in the same breath as naming it.
  • Motifs need a named carrier. "Repeated earworm melody" invites Suno to add a voice to sing it; "repeating cello motif" doesn't.
  • Film-score and ballad anchors suppress the drum kit, and the anchor sits earlier in the prompt than the kit does, so it wins. Use the Drums first toggle to hoist the whole rhythm block ahead of the anchor.
  • Separators change how Suno reads the prompt. Commas give it discrete items it samples independently; spaces let adjacent words fuse into compound descriptors. The two prompts that have worked best for you are the space-separated ones. Past roughly eight comma-separated elements, priority collapses and the output averages out.
  • Exclusions belong in the Exclude Styles field, not as negations inside the style box.
  • v4.5 is inventive but ignores specifications. v5 obeys them. Explore in 4.5, execute in 5.
  • Heavy swing reads as happy. For serious material use laid-back timing and ghost notes, and keep tambourine and handclaps out.
